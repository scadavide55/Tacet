The quiet browser
for the Mac.

Tacet is a small, fast browser built on WebKit, the engine behind Safari. It blocks ads and trackers before they load, keeps your tabs tidy, and has an assistant that reads the page and can run entirely on your Mac. No account, no analytics, free and open source.

Switching from Arc

Arc stops working on 22 December. Your sidebar moves in a minute.

The Browser Company is ending Arc on the Mac and Windows, and after that day it won't open. Tacet reads Arc's sidebar straight from your Mac and rebuilds it: every Space as a tab group, Favorites as pinned tabs, folders as groups of their own, with your history, bookmarks and sign-ins. Imported tabs wait folded and load only when you open them.

Features

Safari's engine, the things Safari is missing, and nothing to switch off.

Tabs that tidy themselves

Drag a tab onto another to make a group, or let Tacet group tabs from the same site. Save a group and close it, reopen it later with every tab. Spaces keep work and life apart. Tabs you haven't touched go to sleep and wake up where you left them.

Split view and Reader

Two pages side by side with ⌥⌘S. A cluttered article becomes clean text with ⇧⌘R, and the Reading List keeps it offline.

Passwords with Touch ID

Saved in the Mac's Keychain, filled after Touch ID, strong ones suggested on sign-up. Checked against known breaches. Works beside 1Password.

Tacet Plugins Beta

A store of plugins checked by the developer: Cutout removes picture backgrounds offline, plus a focus timer, smart dark mode, YouTube without Shorts and more.

Extensions

Add the Safari extensions that come with your apps, or Chrome and Firefox ones from a file, on macOS 15.4 or later. You see what each can do first.

Sites as apps

Install any site as an app with its own window and Dock icon, kept apart from your browsing.

Customize Site

Give any site its own theme, font and width, hide floating bars or anything you click on, add your own CSS or JavaScript.

Moving in is easy

Bookmarks, history and sign-ins from Safari, Chrome, Firefox, Brave, Edge, Opera and Arc. Guided setup in English, Italian and French.

Highlights and notes

Mark text on any page in a colour, add a note, find it again later. Search history by what pages said, not only their titles.

For developers

Developer Mode adds the Web Inspector, page source, Responsive Design Mode and user agents. Off until you want it.

Updates itself

New versions are checked, signed and installed for you. Each one opens with a short note on what changed.

Command bar

Addresses, searches, tabs, history and commands, in one bar.

Press ⌘L or ⌘K in Tacet. This is the same bar, with a few tabs and bookmarks made up for it. Type the start of a site and press Tab, a word to search, a sum or a unit, or the name of a command.

Privacy

Ad blocking with a real off switch, and links without their tracking.

Tacet Shield is on from the first launch and works offline. It has a switch for every site, so a page that breaks is one click from working.

  • Works offline. A built-in list covers the biggest ad and tracking networks from the first launch.
  • Community lists. EasyList and EasyPrivacy add tens of thousands of rules, converted to WebKit rules and refreshed weekly.
  • No cookie walls. Consent banners are hidden, optional cookies rejected, and the page scrolls again.
  • Fewer YouTube ads. Video ads are stripped or skipped.
  • Third-party cookies blocked and fingerprinting made harder, so sites can't follow you around.

The switches on the right work. ⇧⌘B turns Shield off and on in Tacet.

Links arrive without their tracking tags.

Shared links carry tags that tell advertisers where you came from. Tacet strips them before the page loads, so you land on the same page, minus the tracking.

  • Removes utm_, fbclid, gclid, msclkid and dozens more, including site-specific ones on YouTube, Spotify, Amazon and X
  • “Copy Link Without Tracking” in every page's menu
  • Upgrades to HTTPS, sends Global Privacy Control, and private windows lock with Touch ID

Tacet Assistant

Ask the page you're on.

⇧⌘A opens a panel beside any page, and the page comes along. The assistant reads your other tabs, history and bookmarks, the transcript of a YouTube video, and looks things up on the web, showing each step as it works.

  • Apple's on-device model on macOS 26: free, offline, no key. Or your own key for Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter or DeepSeek, or a local model in Ollama or LM Studio.
  • Web access searches and reads pages, with the sources under the answer.
  • Deep research Beta plans, searches from several angles, reads pages in full and writes a report with its sources.
  • Browser use Beta is off until you turn it on. It never touches password or card fields, and asks before sending, buying or deleting.
  • Chat history stays on your Mac. Text in web pages is treated as content, never as instructions.

Questions

Before you download.

I use Arc. What comes over, and when should I move?

Your Spaces become tab groups, Favorites become pinned tabs and folders become groups of their own, along with history, bookmarks and sign-ins. Passwords come over from a CSV you export in Arc. Arc stops working on 22 December 2026 and won't open after that, so import before then. The full guide lists everything, including what Tacet doesn't have.

Is Tacet free?

Yes. Tacet is free to download and use, and open source under the GPL-3.0 license. It's made by one person.

Does Tacet collect any data?

No. There are no analytics and no accounts. Tacet only talks to the sites you visit. If you turn them on, it also downloads filter lists from easylist.to and fetches search suggestions from your search engine; suggestions are off by default. Tacet Assistant is off too. If you turn it on, your questions and the page you attach go only to the AI provider you chose, with your own key. When it searches the web for you, the search goes to your provider (Anthropic) or to DuckDuckGo (other providers). With Apple's on-device model, nothing leaves your Mac.

Why does macOS warn me when I open it?

Tacet isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS may say Tacet “is damaged and can't be opened”. The download is fine. Open Terminal and run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Tacet.app once, then open Tacet as usual. Tacet's own updates keep working after that, and each one is checked against its signature.

Which engine does Tacet use?

WebKit, the same engine as Safari. Sites look and behave the way they do in Safari, and you get its speed and battery life. It's not built on Chromium.

What does the assistant cost? Is it still a beta?

Nothing from Tacet. With Apple's on-device model on macOS 26 it's free and offline. With Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter or DeepSeek you pay the provider for what you use with your own key. With Ollama or LM Studio the model runs on your Mac. The assistant itself isn't a beta any more; Deep research and Browser use still are.

Does Tacet support extensions?

Yes, on macOS 15.4 or later. Tacet runs web extensions, the same kind Safari, Chrome and Firefox use. Add the Safari extensions that come with apps on your Mac in one click from Settings › Extensions, or add a Chrome (.crx), Firefox (.xpi) or unpacked extension from a file. Most work; a few features only Chrome or Firefox have may not. Extensions never run in private windows.

What are Tacet Plugins?

Small plugins made for Tacet and checked by its developer, in a store inside the browser (⇧⌘P). They work on every Mac, are signed and checked file by file before they install, and run in a space of their own that pages can't reach. Tacet Plugins is in beta.

Why did Lumen become Tacet?

There were already other browsers called Lumen, and the name sounded too much like an AI product. Tacet is the mark in music for silence, where an instrument stays quiet. It's the same browser: updating from Lumen keeps your tabs, passwords, settings and extensions.

Does it block every YouTube ad?

Most of them, most of the time. YouTube changes its ads often, so some may still get through.
