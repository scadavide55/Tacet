# Tacet Guide

Everything Tacet Assistant knows about Tacet. Each section starts with "## ". Menu paths use the English names; the app's menus follow the Mac's language (Italian and French are translated).

## About Tacet: what it is, who made it, how, engine, license
Tacet is a free, open-source web browser for the Mac, "the quiet browser for your Mac": minimal, fast and private. It is built on WebKit, the same engine as Safari, so sites behave as in Safari, with Safari's speed and battery life; it is not based on Chromium.
- Made by one independent developer, scadavide55 on GitHub. It is open source under the GNU General Public License v3.0 (GPL-3.0).
- Written in Swift with SwiftUI and AppKit, on WebKit's WKWebView. It builds with a small script (build.sh) and the Xcode command-line tools, as a universal app for Apple silicon and Intel.
- Requirements: macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Web extensions need macOS 15.4; page translation needs macOS 15; Apple's on-device model needs macOS 26 with Apple Intelligence.
- Website: https://scadavide55.github.io/Tacet/ · Code and releases: https://github.com/scadavide55/Tacet · Report a bug: Help › Report a Bug (opens GitHub Issues).
- Tacet has no accounts and no analytics. It talks only to the sites you visit, plus things you turn on (filter lists, search suggestions, the AI provider you choose, Tacet Plugins, update checks on GitHub).
- The current version is shown in Tacet › About Tacet and in Settings › General.

## Why Lumen became Tacet (the name)
Tacet used to be called Lumen, and Tacet Assistant used to be called Lumen Assistant. The name changed in version 1.6.2 because there were already other browsers called Lumen, and the name sounded too much like an AI product. "Tacet" is the mark in music for silence, where an instrument stays quiet, like the ads and trackers Tacet keeps out. The icon, "Eclissi", is an eclipse: a dark disc in front of the sun leaving a crescent of light.
Updating from Lumen keeps everything: tabs, passwords, AI key, settings, history, bookmarks and extensions. After quitting once, Lumen.app in Applications is renamed Tacet.app. The project moved to github.com/scadavide55/Tacet.

## Command bar
Press ⌘L or ⌘K, or click the address. Typing the start of a site you visit often completes it in grey (Tab or → accepts, Esc dismisses, Return opens it); this can be turned off in Settings › Search. Sums (12*7+3) and unit conversions (10 km, 21 °C to F, 70 kg, 5 ft in cm) are worked out on the spot; an address already open in another tab is offered first as Switch to Tab. Type an address, a search, the name of an open tab, a bookmark, something from your history or a command (for example "dark", "pip", "plugins", "sort tabs"). ↩ opens, ⌘↩ opens in a new tab, ↑↓ select, esc closes. Search shortcuts: type a keyword, a space and the search, such as "yt lofi beats" (YouTube), "w" (Wikipedia), "gh" (GitHub), "maps", "r" (Reddit); add your own in Settings › Search. Type a sum, such as 12*7+3 or (4,5 + 2) / 3, and its result shows below the search: ↓ then ↩ copies it (+ − × ÷ ^ and brackets; a comma works as a decimal point). With the assistant on, "Ask Tacet Assistant" gives a quick answer right in the command bar (see the assistant section).

## Tabs, sidebar and layouts
- Layouts (Settings › Appearance, or the setup): Sidebar (vertical tabs, resizable), Separate (tabs along the top) or Compact. The sidebar can be on the left or right, and can hold the address bar. Hide it with ⌘S; hover the window's edge to peek at it. With tabs along the top (Separate or Compact), tabs that don't fit run off the right: scroll over them with a trackpad or a mouse wheel to reach them.
- Pinned tabs sit at the top as icons: right-click a tab › Pin. Drag tabs to reorder.
- Hovering over a tab shows a preview: the page as you last saw it, its site and how much memory it uses (or that it's sleeping). Turn previews off in Settings › General › Tabs.
- Search your tabs with ⌥⌘F (Tab › Search Tabs…); Return opens the first match.
- Background tabs hibernate to save memory (Settings › General › Hibernate background tabs) and are shown in grey. When you open one, it shows the page as you left it while it loads, then scrolls back to where you were. Tab › Hibernate Other Tabs frees memory now.
- Pick several tabs with ⌘-click and ⇧-click, then right-click to group, move, pin or close them together, or drag them.
- Tab menu: next/previous, pin, duplicate, mute, hibernate others, group by site, search tabs, close duplicate tabs, sort tabs (by site, title or last opened), saved groups.
- Move tabs or whole groups to another window or profile: right-click › Move to Window.
- Pinned tabs keep their site, as in Arc: links to other sites open in a new tab (Settings › General › Tabs › Pinned tabs open links to other sites in new tabs); right-click a pinned tab › Back to Pinned Address returns to where it was pinned.
- The tab list works from the keyboard: View › Move to Tab List (⌥⌘↓), or Tab until a ring shows on a tab; ↑ ↓ move the ring, Return opens that tab, ⌘⌫ closes it.
- Closing several tabs at once (Close Other Tabs, Close Tabs Below, a group, picked tabs, duplicates) shows "3 tabs closed · Undo" at the bottom of the page for a few seconds; Undo brings them back in their places.
- ⇧⌘T reopens the last closed tab, or all the tabs closed together (Close Other Tabs, Close Tabs Below, closing a group or several picked tabs); History › Recently Closed lists them one by one. Middle-click (the scroll wheel) on a tab closes it.
- Close tabs automatically after a day, week or month (Settings › General › Tabs). Restore windows and tabs when Tacet opens is on by default.

## Tab groups
Drag a tab onto another to make a group, or right-click › Add to New Group. Click a group's name to fold or unfold it; double-click to rename it; right-click for New Tab in Group, Colour, Never Group This Site, Ungroup Tabs, Save and Close Group, Close Group and Move Group to Window. Tacet can group tabs from the same site automatically (Settings › General › Organize tabs: Automatically by site, when a site has 2, 3 or 5 tabs), except sites you exclude; Tab › Group Tabs by Site does it once. Saved groups come back from Tab › Saved Groups with all their tabs. Tacet Assistant can group tabs by topic when you ask ("put my Rome trip tabs in a group").

## Spaces
A Space is a whole set of tabs, pinned tabs, groups and split view, with a note to yourself, that a window leaves and comes back to in one step: one for School, one for Coding, and switching swaps everything at once. The Spaces are the icons at the bottom of the sidebar: click one to switch, + makes a new one (also Tab › New Space… and the command bar's New Space). ⌃⌘→ and ⌃⌘← go to the next and previous Space; Tab › Spaces lists them, and the command bar's "Switch to" opens one. The first time, the tabs you already had become a Space called Main. Each Space belongs to the profile of the window it was made in, and only opens in that profile's windows, so sign-ins never cross profiles. The Space's name and note show at the top of the sidebar: click the note to edit it ("pick up from exercise 4"). Right-click a Space's icon to rename it, edit its note, choose its icon and colour, move it, delete it, or turn Spaces off. With tabs along the top, the Space button's menu does the same. Spaces only appear once you make one. To stop using them: Turn Off Spaces… (in that menu, Tab menu, the command bar, or Settings › General › Spaces): every Space is removed and nothing is lost, as the tabs of the other Spaces come into the window, each Space as a tab group. Deleting the Space you're in keeps the tabs you see. A Space open in another window is brought forward instead of opening twice. Pages reload when you come back to them, as after a restart. Private windows don't use Spaces.

## Link preview
⌥-click a link (or right-click it › Open Link in Preview) to open it in a panel over the page, without leaving the page. Esc or a click outside closes it; Open as Tab (⌘↩) keeps it as a tab; Side by Side shows it next to the page in split view. Switching tab closes the preview.

## Highlights and notes
Select text on a page, right-click › Highlight, and pick a colour (yellow, green, blue, pink, purple), or Highlight with Note… to add a note. Highlights come back when you open the page again, found by their words. Right-click a highlight to edit its note or remove it. Library › Highlights lists them all with their notes and pages: search them, open one (the page scrolls to the passage), copy as Markdown, change colour, delete, or Export as Markdown… for Obsidian or other notes apps. A highlight stays in the Library even if the page changes. Not in private windows.

## Opening rules
Settings › General › Opening rules: choose where links to a site open. Switch to its open tab (a pinned one first), open in a private window, use a profile, or open in a Space. Rules apply to links from other apps, addresses typed in the command bar and links opened in a new tab (⌘-click, or links that open a new tab); never to links inside a page or sign-in windows. Right-click › Open Link in New Tab opens a link where you are, whatever the rules. Subdomains match too (github.com covers gist.github.com).

## Recovering unsent text
When you write long text in a page (a comment, a message, a form) and the page reloads, crashes or the tab closes before you send it, Tacet offers it back the next time you open the page: Put It Back puts it in its field, Copy copies it, Discard forgets it. Never passwords, card numbers or one-time codes, never private windows, and nothing older than a week. A sent form or an emptied field forgets it. Turn it off in Settings › Shield › Keep unsent text to put it back.

## Automations
Settings › Automations: lists of steps that run in order, the same way every time, with no AI or key. Steps: open a page in a new tab or this tab, show a page beside this one, switch to a Space, copy title, link and selection as Markdown, open the selected links in a new group, Reader, Focus Mode, close the other tabs, put the other tabs to sleep, dark, light or system appearance. Start from + or the examples (Copy as Markdown note, Collect selected links, Just this page, Clear the decks). Run one from the command bar (type its name), Tab › Automations, Run Now in Settings, or give it a shortcut from ⌃⌥⌘1 to ⌃⌥⌘9.

## Windows, profiles and private browsing
- Private windows (⇧⌘N) forget history, cookies and site data when you close them. Settings › Shield can lock private windows with Touch ID when Tacet is in the background; ⌃⌘L locks any window now.
- Profiles keep their own cookies, sign-ins and site data, so you can be signed in to different accounts at once; history, bookmarks, passwords and settings are shared. Create one in Settings › General › Profiles › Add Profile, rename it, pick its colour, and press Open Window. File › New Window in Profile opens one too.
- When you have profiles, Tacet asks "Who's browsing?" when it opens (unless it reopens your last windows or opens from a link); turn it off with "Ask which profile to use when Tacet opens" in Settings › General.
- A profile can connect through its own proxy: Settings › General › Profiles, the network button of the profile, then SOCKS5 or HTTP (CONNECT), host and port (1–65535; until both are right, it connects directly). The profile's row says Proxy host:port, and its windows' profile badge in the sidebar shows a network icon and the proxy. Only that profile's windows use it; private windows and the main profile never do. There's no proxy user name or password.
- Split view (⌥⌘S, or right-click a tab › Open in Split View) shows two pages side by side.

## Tacet Shield (ad and tracker blocking)
Built-in blocking of ads and trackers with WebKit's content blocker, before they load, offline. Turn it on or off everywhere with ⇧⌘B, or for one site from the shield button in the toolbar (Toggle Shield for This Site). The shield's panel shows how many ads and trackers were blocked on this page, on this site and since Tacet opened, and from which sites. Options (shield panel, Shield menu, Settings › Shield):
- hide cookie consent banners, and reject optional cookies for you (presses "Reject all");
- skip YouTube ads;
- fingerprinting protection: canvas, WebGL and audio readings change slightly per site, the exact graphics chip and core count are hidden;
- block third-party cookies; remove tracking parameters (utm_, fbclid, gclid…) from links;
- optional filter lists, updated weekly: EasyList, EasyPrivacy, AdGuard Base, Fanboy Annoyances and AdGuard's hidden (CNAME) trackers.
Sites where the shield is off are listed in Settings › Shield.
Site not working? (in the shield's panel, when the shield is on for the page) turns the shield off on that site for now and reloads the page; a banner then asks whether it works: Keep Off adds the site to the sites where the shield is off, Turn Shield Back On blocks again and reloads, and closing the banner leaves the shield off on the site until Tacet quits. Nothing is sent anywhere.

## Privacy and security
Upgrades connections to HTTPS (with an HTTPS-only option; a site with a broken certificate is never quietly opened over HTTP: Tacet explains and lets you choose), sends Global Privacy Control, warns about fraudulent sites (Apple's Safe Browsing), asks before downloading apps, installers and scripts, can clear cookies, cache and history when Tacet quits, and clears browsing data by time range (Settings › Shield). Favicons are never fetched from your local network. Tacet updates are signed: it installs only updates signed with its developer's key and certificate.

Site Information: click the lock (or right-click the address › Site Information…, or "Site Information" in the command bar) for one panel with the connection and certificate, the camera and microphone choices for the site (Allow, Block or Forget; the same list as Settings › Sites), zoom, "Pop-ups without a click" (turning it off removes the site from the allowed list), Tacet Shield on this site with what it blocked on the page, and Clear Data for This Site.

File fields: when a page asks for a file, the open panel offers only the types the field accepts (for example images or PDFs); if the page opens it without a click, every file is offered.

Location: sites can't see your location in Tacet. WebKit lets a browser answer a site's location request only through a part of WebKit Apple keeps private, so a maps site's "Show my location" gets no location: the site shows an error or keeps waiting. Type the place instead.

## Passwords
Tacet saves passwords in the Mac's Keychain and fills them after Touch ID or your Mac password (Settings › Passwords). It can suggest strong passwords on sign-up pages, import and export CSV for Apple Passwords and other managers, and work alongside 1Password, KeePassXC, Bitwarden and others (Password manager picker). Password check (Settings › Passwords › Check…) finds passwords found in data breaches (via Have I Been Pwned; only the first 5 characters of each password's SHA-1 hash are sent), reused on several sites, or easy to guess.

Passkeys: Tacet can't use passkeys yet, because Apple gives the permission they need only to browsers in its developer program. When a site asks for a passkey, the site shows an error and a Tacet banner offers Sign In with Safari: sign in there with Touch ID, come back and click I'm Signed In, and Tacet copies that site's sign-in cookies from Safari and reloads the page. It needs Full Disk Access to read Safari's sign-ins. Close the banner to use another way to sign in.
## Importing from other browsers and moving in
File › Import from Another Browser… (or the setup) brings bookmarks, history and sign-ins (cookies) from Safari, Chrome, Firefox, Opera, Brave, Edge, Arc, Vivaldi, Zen and other Chromium or Firefox browsers. From Arc it also brings Spaces, Favorites, pinned tabs and folders: Favorites become pinned tabs, each Space and folder a tab group that loads only when opened (Arc stops working on December 22, 2026). Passwords come from a CSV export (Settings › Passwords › Import from CSV). Safari's data needs Full Disk Access for Tacet. Make Tacet your default browser from the setup or System Settings.

## Reading, translation, listening and Focus Mode
- Reader (⇧⌘R, or the page icon in the address) shows a clean version of an article.
- Reading List: Bookmarks › Save for Offline keeps the Reader version with its images on your Mac; open saved articles from Bookmarks › Show Reading List or the Library.
- Translate Page (⌥⌘T, View menu or the ⋯ menu) translates on your Mac with Apple's translation (macOS 15+): nothing leaves your Mac; Show Original puts it back.
- Read Aloud (⌥⌘A) reads the article with the Mac's voices.
- Focus Mode (⇧⌘J) hides everything but the page; move the pointer to the top to exit.
- Find on page (⌘F): shows which match of how many ("3 of 12"), highlights every match, narrows as you type; the options button has Match Case and Whole Words; ⌘G and ⇧⌘G go to the next and previous match, ⌘E searches for the selected text. Per-site zoom, print, export as PDF, screenshot of the page.

## Screenshots
File › Take Screenshot of Page (⇧⌘S) takes what's visible; Take Screenshot of Whole Page (⌥⇧⌘S) takes the whole page, top to bottom, drawn in pieces for very long pages (up to 40,000 points tall; a huge one is drawn at a lower resolution); Take Screenshot of Element… lets you click the part to take, highlighted as in Customize Site (↑ for the block around it, Esc to cancel). The ⋯ menu › Take Screenshot and the command bar have all three. Each is copied to the clipboard and offered to save as a PNG in Downloads.

## Customize Site
The ⋯ menu › Customize This Site… gives any site its own light or dark theme, font, page width, hidden floating bars and images, reduced motion, elements you hide by clicking them (Pick Elements to Hide: ↑ selects a bigger block, Esc finishes), and your own CSS and JavaScript. Changes show as you make them. All customized sites are in Settings › Sites. Settings › Sites also has Force dark pages, default and per-site zoom, and camera and microphone permissions.

## Developer Mode
Settings › Advanced › Developer Mode (or "Turn On Developer Mode" in the command bar) adds the Develop menu, in the menu bar and in the page's ⋯ menu, and Inspect Element, View Page Source and Page Info to the page's right-click menu. The Develop menu has: Show Web Inspector (⌥⌘I, again to close it; it opens in its own window), Show JavaScript Console (⌥⌘C), Show Page Resources, Inspect Element… (⌥⇧⌘C, then click an element), View Page Source (⌥⌘U, in its own window, coloured, ⌘F to search), Page Info (load times, requests and size, protocol, elements, scripts, cookies, local storage, viewport), Responsive Design Mode (the page at the size of an iPhone SE, iPhone 16, iPhone 16 Pro Max, Pixel 9, iPad mini, iPad Air, a small laptop or a desktop, with that device's user agent, or a Custom Size… you type, such as 390 × 844; the arrows button turns it sideways, ✕ ends it), User Agent (Safari, Chrome, Firefox or Edge on other systems, for the current tab), Open Page With (another browser on this Mac), Disable JavaScript (for the current tab), Disable Styles, Show Element Outlines, Edit Page Text (type anywhere on the page), Empty Caches (⌥⌘E) and Clear Data for This Site… (cookies, storage and caches of that site only, which signs you out of it). Typing view-source: followed by an address in the command bar shows that page's HTML (fetched without your cookies). Safari's Develop menu can also inspect Tacet's pages while Developer Mode is on.

## Tacet Plugins (beta)
A store of small plugins made for Tacet and checked by its developer, that work on every Mac. Open it with ⇧⌘P (Window › Tacet Plugins), the store button at the bottom of the sidebar, the puzzle button in the toolbar, the command bar ("Tacet Plugins") or Settings › Extensions › Open Tacet Plugins. Each card says who made the plugin and whether it's "Verified by Tacet"; Add installs it, the switch turns it on or off, Remove removes it, and plugins update by themselves. Plugins are signed and checked file by file, and run in their own space that pages can't reach. Cutout (made by scadavide55, scadavide55.github.io/cutout) removes the background of any picture on this Mac: rest the pointer on a picture and click ✂ Cutout or press ⌥⇧X, or open it from its card or the command bar ("Open Cutout") and drop photos; its four AI engines (413 MB) download once when you add it, then it works offline, at tacet-plugin://cutout/. Focus Timer is a focus timer in a tab of its own (25 + 5 minutes, or your lengths) with the time left in the tab's title. Smart Dark Mode darkens light sites and leaves dark ones alone; Clean Links on Copy removes tracking from copied links (⌥⇧L copies the page's clean address); Bigger YouTube Captions enlarges subtitles. Right-click any picture › Remove Background with Cutout. Other plugins include YouTube without Shorts, YouTube Focus, Copy as Markdown (⌥⇧C), Reading Progress, Hide Comments, Video Speed ([ ] and \), Picture in Picture (⌥⇧P), Keyboard Navigation (F for link hints, J/K to scroll), Back to Top, Word Count (⌥⇧W), Other Sites in New Tabs, AMP to Original, Google Just Results, Amazon Without Sponsored, X Without Distractions, Stack Overflow Clean, GitHub Wide and Old Reddit.

## Extensions menu (puzzle button)
The puzzle button in the toolbar, next to the shield, lists every installed Tacet plugin and web extension in alphabetical order, with its icon, name and a green dot when it's on. Clicking one does what it's for: Cutout opens; action plugins run their action on the page (Copy as Markdown, Count Words, Float the Video, Show Link Hints); on/off plugins (such as Hide Comments or YouTube without Shorts) turn off; Video Speed shows how to use it; web extensions open their popup. Cutout also has an Open button. A plugin that's off is grey, and clicking it turns it back on. The ⓘ button shows how to use each one, with its shortcut. The pin button (or right-click › Pin to Toolbar) puts a plugin or extension in the toolbar as its own button. Right-click a plugin to turn it off for the current site only (Turn Off for example.com), off everywhere, or remove it. Style-only plugins turn on and off on open pages at once; others reload the page you're on. Installed plugins update by themselves when Tacet opens. Arrows and Return work, Esc closes. "Tacet Plugins…" opens the store; the store button at the bottom of the sidebar does too.

## Web extensions
On macOS 15.4 or later, Settings › Extensions adds web extensions, the same kind Safari, Chrome and Firefox use: the Safari extensions that come with your apps in one click, or a folder, .zip, .crx (Chrome) or .xpi (Firefox) file. Tacet shows what an extension can do before adding it. Their buttons appear next to the shield, with badges and popups; right-click menu items and settings pages work too. Extensions never run in private windows; turning one off closes its pages, and removing one deletes its data. Tacet remembers what you allowed: if an extension inside another app updates and asks for more, Tacet asks you again before granting it, and remembers a no.

## Sites as apps
File › Install Site as App… makes any site an app with its own window, Dock icon and cookies, in ~/Applications/Tacet Apps. These apps update together with Tacet.

## Appearance and start page
Settings › Appearance: light, dark or system appearance, any accent colour, window tint that follows the site's colour, floating page with rounded corners, compact tabs, sidebar position, full address, link addresses on hover, pull down at the top of a page to reload. The start page has a clock, a greeting, favourites and backgrounds: gradients, animated gradients, your own photo or a short video (Settings › Appearance › Start page background, and Settings › General › Start page).
App icon (Settings › Appearance › App icon, or the "Choose an icon" step of the setup): Tacet's own icon is Seal, corona, a vermilion seal with a corona of rays carved in it. Sixteen alternatives can replace it: Gold seal; Half seal; Seal, t.; Half seal, t.; t. coin; t., ocean; t., half lit; Half moon; Half moon, t; Half moon, soft; Half moon, corona; Corona, gold; Corona t; Corona t, gold; Last light; and Eclipse (until 1.6.4), the icon Tacet had until 1.6.4. The choice shows in the Dock at once and is set on Tacet.app, so Finder and the Dock show it while Tacet is closed too; after an update it comes back at the next launch. Sites installed as apps keep their own icons.

## Search
Settings › Search: the search engine (DuckDuckGo, Google, Bing, Brave, Startpage, Ecosia, Kagi or your own with %s), search suggestions (off by default, never in private windows) and search shortcuts.

## Library: history, bookmarks, downloads, Reading List
⌘Y opens history, ⌥⌘B bookmarks, ⌥⌘L downloads, and the Reading List is in the Library too.
Downloads: Window › Downloads (⌥⌘L), or the downloads button in the toolbar or sidebar (it appears once there's a download) lists them with their size, speed and time left; the Dock icon shows how many are running. Each one can be paused and resumed (if the site allows it), cancelled (which deletes the part already saved), downloaded again, opened, opened with another app, copied (to paste the file in Finder or a message), shown in Finder, dragged out to Finder or another app, removed from the list or moved to the Trash: right-click a download for every option. Pause All and Resume All are at the top, Clear removes the finished ones. Quitting Tacet while files are downloading asks first, then pauses them so they resume when Tacet opens again. The list is kept when Tacet restarts (downloads from private windows leave it when the last private window closes, and are never saved) for a week by default (Settings › General › Downloads › Remove from the downloads list: after one day, one week, one month or manually), and clearing history clears it too; the files themselves stay. Library › Downloads has the whole list with a search field. In the list, ↑ ↓ choose a download, the space bar shows it in Quick Look (again to close), Return opens it; right-click › Quick Look works too. A tab opened only for a file (a link that opens a new tab, an address from another app) closes once the download starts. Settings › General › Downloads › Ask where to save each download; apps, installers and scripts ask before downloading (Settings › Shield). History › Clear History removes the last hour, today or everything. Searching history (History, or the command bar) looks through every page ever visited, not only the newest, with a full-text index on titles and addresses: each word matches by its start and accents don't matter. Results are ranked by frecency: pages visited often and recently first, pages you typed or chose in the command bar above pages reached by links, and pages on the site you start typing ("git" → github.com) before the rest. The search never runs on the main thread, so typing doesn't wait. Bookmarks import and export as HTML.
Find pages by what they said: Tacet keeps the text of the pages you visit, on this Mac only, so searching History (or typing three letters or more in the command bar, results marked Read) finds a page by a sentence you remember, with the matching passage. Words match by their start, and accents don't matter. Never kept: private windows, pages with a password field, and sites you exclude. Settings › Shield › Searching what pages said: turn it off (which deletes what was kept), keep it for 1 month, 3 months (default) or 1 year, exclude sites, or Forget All. Right-click a result › Forget What This Page Said. Clearing history clears it too. No AI and no network.

## Tacet Assistant
An AI assistant in a panel beside the page (⇧⌘A), or full-window. It was called Lumen Assistant before the rename. It is off until you turn it on in Settings › AI, and nothing is sent until you ask. Deep research and Browser use are still in beta.
- Providers: your own key from Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter or DeepSeek; Ollama or LM Studio running models on your Mac with no key (Settings › AI finds their models by itself, says which read pictures, and tells you when the app isn't running; their server address can be changed); any other OpenAI-compatible server; or Apple's on-device model (macOS 26 with Apple Intelligence: free, offline, private, but smaller and less reliable). Keys stay in the Keychain. Pick the model in Settings › AI or in the chat.
- It reads the page you're on, your other tabs, your history and bookmarks, YouTube transcripts, and can take a screenshot of the page; it opens pages and searches, switches tabs and groups tabs.
- Web access (on by default) lets it search the web and read pages, listing sources. Browser use (off by default, the button below the chat box) lets it click, type, scroll and drag on the page; it never fills passwords or card numbers, and Tacet itself asks before its clicks, whatever the model was told: always before one that could send, buy, delete or publish (a form's submit button, or a button whose words say so), and before any other button, since a page decides what its buttons do; plain links don't ask. Allow on This Page lets it use the other buttons on that page until the page changes or you send a new request. Stop ends it at once, even in the middle of several steps.
- It uses your Tacet Plugins when you ask: "remove the background of this image" opens Cutout with the page's main picture, "copy this page as Markdown" runs Copy as Markdown, and it opens Focus Timer or other plugin apps.
- Select text and right-click › Tacet Assistant › Explain, Translate, Summarise or Ask About It.
- In the command bar, type a question and choose "Ask Tacet Assistant" for a quick answer that searches the web only when it needs to (and never when Web access is off); ✕ closes it, the arrows continue it in the full chat. Quick answers use a short prompt and open the connection while you're still typing the question, so they start sooner; with Anthropic they use Claude Haiku 5.5 and with OpenAI GPT-5 mini (Settings › AI › Answers › Faster answers in the command bar turns this off).
- Answers are short and to the point by default, in a calm voice with a dry sense of humour now and then. Settings › AI › Answers › Length can make them Detailed, and turning off Personality makes the voice neutral.
- Deep research (the binoculars button below the chat box, next to Web access and Browser use) is for questions that need a thorough answer. The small menu beside it sets how far it goes: Quick (about 4 sources, a minute or two), Standard (about 8 sources, checked against each other) or Thorough (about 12 sources, and a second search for what's still missing). With every provider but Anthropic, Tacet runs the research step by step: it plans a few sub-questions, runs their searches at the same time, chooses the most authoritative pages, reads them a few at a time, takes short notes from each page with the exact words that back them (a note whose words aren't really in the page is dropped), counts a fact as confirmed only when two different sites back it, notes where sources disagree, and writes the report from the notes alone, with a direct answer first, sections, its sources linked inline and a section on what remains uncertain. With Anthropic's models the research runs on Anthropic's servers, with the same kind of report. The line above the reply counts as it goes (Reading · 6 of 10 pages, Notes from 6 sources). Stop ends it at any step and shows what it had found so far, marked as stopped. Its notes stay in the chat, so a follow-up question can build on them. Export Report below a research saves it as Markdown or PDF with its sources at the bottom. It turns Web access on, stays on for the chat until you turn it off; a new chat starts with it off. It isn't available with Apple's on-device model.
- The line above a reply shows what the assistant is doing right now (Searching the web, Reading 3 pages, Using the browser, Thinking, Going through the results, Writing the answer, or the stage of a deep research), with three pulsing dots on the left and the time so far on the right. Click it to open the list of steps: each search, page read, step on a page or research stage, whether it worked, what it found (such as 8 results) and how long it took. Click a search or a page in the list to open it in a new tab. Once the reply is done, the line sums it up (Searched the web, read 4 pages) with the total time; an orange mark means a step didn't work. Thinking shows only as time spent, never its content.
- Replies show headings, lists, tables, quotes and code blocks (with a Copy button); links in a reply open in a new tab. Sources the answer used are listed as small chips below it. Below a finished reply, Copy copies it and Ask Again (on the last reply) asks for it once more.
- Searches and page reads in the same step run at the same time. If the AI service is busy or the connection drops before the reply starts, Tacet tries again twice by itself.
- Links are checked: a link in a reply to a page the assistant never read or saw in the conversation still opens, but it is dimmed with a small question mark ("Source not read by the assistant"); a deep research report ends with how many there were. The check is saved with the chat.
- It reads PDFs from the web (up to 25 MB): it knows the page count, marks each page, and says which page a long PDF was cut at; the step reads "PDF · 34 pages". Pages that show their text only with JavaScript are read again in a hidden private window with Tacet Shield on (nothing saved, private addresses blocked, 12 seconds at most; skipped when JavaScript is off in Settings); the step reads "Read with rendering".
- Web search: Settings › AI › Web access can add a SearXNG server (it can be your own) or a Brave Search API key (kept in the Keychain), in the order you choose with the arrows. The assistant tries them in that order and moves on when one fails; DuckDuckGo needs no setup and is used when nothing else answers. When DuckDuckGo blocks automated searches, Tacet waits briefly and tries once more. The step says which engine answered (8 results · Brave). At most three searches run at once.
- Models on your Mac: Tacet reads how much text the model can take from Ollama or LM Studio and shows it in Settings › AI (Context: 16,384 tokens), with a warning when it's small for deep research. Ollama gets the context with each request (up to 16,384 tokens unless the model's Modelfile sets num_ctx), so the instructions are never cut off; LM Studio uses the context the model was loaded with. A model with less than about 16,000 tokens gets short instructions and only the tools it needs, so a page still fits. Long conversations and long research are shortened to fit any model: pages read earlier are cut first, never the latest ones, and a cut page says how much of it arrived.
- Personal instructions (Settings › AI): what the assistant should know about you and how it should answer. Private windows leave them out unless you turn on Use them in private windows.
- Memory (Settings › AI › Memory, off by default): turn on Remember things between chats and the assistant can offer to remember a short fact; it shows the exact text and saves it only if you click Save. Memories are listed there to edit or delete (or Delete All Memories…), stay on this Mac, go into the encrypted backup, and are never used or saved in private windows.
- Type @ in the message box to pick open tabs (up to 5): they're read and sent with your question, shown as chips.
- Settings › AI › Model for small jobs: a smaller, cheaper model that names your chats and does the small steps of a deep research (planning, choosing sources, notes). With none chosen, the default model does them, and chats are named after your first question.
- Under each answer, Tacet shows the tokens it used (in, out, cached) when the provider reports them; it shows no price.
- Chats are saved on your Mac (never in private windows) and listed in the chat history. Search chats (in the history and the full-window chat list) finds a chat by any word said in it, accents ignored, and shows the matching passage. Right-click a saved chat to Pin Chat (pinned chats stay at the top), Export as Markdown… or Delete Chat; the share button saves the current chat as Markdown with its sources at the bottom.
- It can't change Tacet's settings itself; it explains where they are.

## Moving to another Mac
Settings › General › Move to another Mac › Export Settings and Plugins… saves one .tacetsettings file with your settings, search shortcuts, site styles, profiles and Tacet Plugins (on, off, pinned, sites where they're off). Import… on the other Mac reinstalls the plugins from Tacet Plugins (verified), applies the settings and restarts Tacet. Passwords, AI keys, history and bookmarks are not in the file: export passwords as CSV (Settings › Passwords) and bookmarks as HTML (Library).

Back Up Everything… (Settings › General › Move to another Mac) makes one encrypted .tacetbackup file with all of Tacet: settings and plugins, open tabs and Spaces, history, bookmarks, Reading List with its saved articles, highlights, saved groups, rules, automations, page text, saved passwords (after Touch ID), AI keys and the cookies of the sites you're signed in to, for every profile. You choose a password (at least 8 characters, typed twice); the file is encrypted with it (AES-GCM) and can't be opened or recovered without it. Restore from Backup… on the other Mac asks for the password, replaces everything in Tacet with the backup and restarts. Icons and Tacet Plugins' files download again by themselves.
## Updates
Tacet checks GitHub for updates once a day (Settings › General › Updates) and can install them for you; Tacet › Check for Updates… checks now. Each version shows a "Welcome to Tacet" screen with what's new, once. Updates are signed, so only real ones install.

## Installing and troubleshooting
Download the .dmg from the website or GitHub, open it and drag Tacet to Applications. Tacet isn't notarized by Apple yet, so the first time macOS may say it "is damaged and can't be opened": the download is fine; run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Tacet.app` once in Terminal, then open it. If macOS asks for Keychain access after an update, choose Always Allow. A page that couldn't open because the Mac was offline loads again by itself when the connection comes back. If a site misbehaves, turn the shield off for that site from the shield button, or try Reload Ignoring Cache (⌥⌘R). Settings (⌘,) has a search field in its toolbar: type part of a setting's name and choose it to open its pane. Report bugs from Help › Report a Bug. Help › Copy Diagnostic Log… shows the events of this session (pages that crashed, Tacet Shield list errors, downloads, update checks) in a window, with addresses cut to their site and files to their type; only Copy puts the text on the clipboard, and nothing is sent anywhere. The same events go to the Mac's log under com.lumen.browser (Console app).
